## Script
**[0:00 Hook]**
"If you've ever balanced an equation, got it 'almost right' and still lost the mark, this video is for you. I'll show you a 5-step method that works every single time. We'll do three equations together, and then I'll give you three to try."

**[0:20 Why balance at all?]**
"Atoms can't appear or disappear in a reaction. That's the law of conservation of mass. So the number of each atom on the left **must** equal the number on the right. A balanced equation is just us counting honestly."

**[0:50 The 5 steps]** *(write on board)*
1. Write the skeletal equation.
2. Make an atom-count table: element, number on the left, number on the right.
3. Start with the **biggest formula** (the one with the most atoms).
4. Change **only the numbers in front** (coefficients). Never touch the small numbers (subscripts).
5. Re-count everything, then add states (s), (l), (g), (aq) if the question asks.

**[1:40 Example 1: iron and steam]**
"Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂. Let's count. Fe: 1 and 3. H: 2 and 2. O: 1 and 4.
The biggest formula is Fe₃O₄. It has 4 oxygens, so put 4 in front of H₂O: Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂.
Now hydrogen: 8 on the left, 2 on the right. Put 4 in front of H₂: Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂.
Finally iron: 1 on the left, 3 on the right, so 3Fe.
**3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂.** Re-count: Fe 3 = 3, H 8 = 8, O 4 = 4 ✓. With states: 3Fe(s) + 4H₂O(g) → Fe₃O₄(s) + 4H₂(g)."

**[3:30 Example 2: the LCM trick]**
"Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃. Oxygen is 2 on the left and 3 on the right. When you see 2 and 3, think LCM, which is 6. 3O₂ gives 6, and 2Al₂O₃ gives 6.
Now aluminium on the right is 2 × 2 = 4, so 4Al.
**4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃** ✓"

**[5:00 Example 3: burning a fuel (C, H, O)]**
"Propane, the gas in some cylinders: C₃H₈ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O.
Rule for fuels: **C first, then H, oxygen last.**
C: 3, so 3CO₂. H: 8, so 4H₂O. Now count oxygen on the right: 3 × 2 + 4 × 1 = 10, so 5O₂.
**C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O** ✓"

**[6:30 Three mistakes that cost marks]**
1. Changing H₂O to H₂O₂ to "make it balance". That's a different substance!
2. Forgetting to re-count after the last change.
3. Skipping states when the question says "with physical states".

**[7:30 Your turn]**
"Pause the video and try these three. The answers are in the pinned comment.
(1) Na + H₂O → NaOH + H₂
(2) KClO₃ → KCl + O₂
(3) C₂H₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O"
