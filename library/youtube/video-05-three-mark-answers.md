## Script
**[0:00 Hook]**
"You studied the chapter. You knew the answer. You wrote a whole paragraph… and got 1.5 out of 3. Sound familiar? Today I'll show you exactly how examiners mark, and the simple formula that gets full marks."

**[0:25 How marking works]**
"Examiners mark with a **marking scheme**: a list of key points. A 3-mark question usually has **3 points, 1 mark each**. If a point is missing, the mark is lost, no matter how long your answer is."

**[1:00 The formula: 3 marks = 3 clear points]**
1. **Read the command word.** *Define* → definition. *Explain* → reason. *Describe* → steps in order. *Differentiate* → a table.
2. **Plan 3 points** before you write (10 seconds, in the margin).
3. **Use the textbook keywords.** These are the words in the marking scheme.
4. **Add an equation, a diagram or an example** when it fits. It often *is* one of the points.
5. **Write point-wise**, not as one block.

**[2:30 Live example]**
*Question:* "Describe how fats are digested in our body. (3 marks)"

*Answer that loses marks (show it on screen):*
"Fats are digested in the stomach and intestine by juices and then they are absorbed in the body."
"What's wrong? There's no bile, no lipase, no fatty acids, and it even mentions the stomach, which isn't where fats are digested. That's about half a mark."

*Full-marks answer (build it point by point):*
1. "Bile from the liver makes the medium alkaline and breaks fats into small globules. This is called **emulsification**."
2. "**Lipase** from the pancreas breaks down the emulsified fats."
3. "Intestinal juice in the small intestine converts fats into **fatty acids and glycerol**."
"Three points, three keywords, three marks ✓"

**[5:00 Second quick example: a 'differentiate' question]**
"'Give three differences between aerobic and anaerobic respiration.' Use a table: oxygen present/absent · mitochondria/cytoplasm · CO₂ + H₂O versus ethanol + CO₂ or lactic acid. One row = one mark."

**[6:00 Five quick rules]**
- Numericals: formula → substitution → answer **with unit**.
- Equations: balanced, with states if asked.
- Diagrams: pencil, neat labels, label lines that don't cross.
- Don't repeat the question back. Start with the answer.
- Leave a line between answers. Examiners love neat papers.

**[7:00 Close]**
"Try it now. Pick any 3-mark question from your book and write it in 3 points. Post your answer in the comments and I'll check a few 🙂 Next video: a 90-day plan to revise the whole Science syllabus before the boards."
